## Brief Description

We propose a project where we will extract article text from a dataset of several thousand news articles pertaining the the 2026 Iran War, and conduct natural language processing based on article semantics in order to determine if natural language analysis can consistently identify ideological leanings or other possible biases present in media reporting on the Iran War. We extracted text from 1925 articles from 77 different sources published within a month (27 Feb 2026 \- 29 Mar 2026). We use an LLM, specifically Gemma 4 E4B, to score each article across 5 dimensions. 

The 5 dimensions are:   
\- kinetic\_focus, relating to military hardware and activity generally,   
\- humanitarian\_focus, relating to concerns for the wellbeing of civilians and other disadvantaged groups,   
\- diplomatic\_focus, relating to how the differing parties are interacting,   
\- economic-focus, relating to the economic impacts of the war, and   
\- culpability\_bias, relating to the extent to which articles use active language and strong verbs to assign moral blame. We would also like to generally examine some of the most high impact words in the sample of articles.

Depending on time availability, we might run similar analyses and scoring methodology on non-US English news sources (e.g. Al Jazeera English, BBC), and 2-3 US outlets’ Ukraine coverage from March 2022\. This would give us cross-conflict and cross geography comparison. 

## proposed visualizations / analyses

We plan to use a combination of text-analysis and network visualizations to show how different media sources cover the war, how that coverage changes over time, and what patterns appear in full article texts. Some of these would focus on the 5 dimensions of framing scores generated by the LLM, and others would directly explore the patterns in the full article texts.

### Some central questions:

Where are the narrative convergences of media reportings of the Iran War? Do they differ / cluster by political lean, by event (same day, same event, same embedding), or by size / type of media outlet, or by region? 

Some hypothesis:  

- Temporal divergence: Are events / shifts during the war visible in how different media outlets frame the narrative trajectories? Which outlets cover the events / shifts the same way? After high-casualty kinetic events, do humanitarian\_focus or kinetic\_focus spike? Do different outlet clusters respond differently to the same event (i.e. is there a casual story)? How do different media sources differ in their emphasis on 5 dimensions of framing? 

- Framing profiles: what variable predicts which cluster an outlet falls into? Are there more clusters between like-minded political leanings, outlet type / size, region, or do they all cluster similarly during certain events? Does economic framing depoliticize the war? (e.g. we can observe if mentions of India’s oil exports, China’s trade routes has a high correlation with ) 

- Text / semantics / sentiment: What do each cluster talk about differently? Which narratives and sentiments are related with which frames? 

- Network: what is the narrative structure of the coverage if we built a network graph where we embed all articles using a sentence transformer? Analysing the timestamps in a network, what would the directed temporal network inform us about contagion across the media outlet ecosystem (i.e. frame diffusion network)? 

### Possible visualizations include:

- **Simple visualizations**  
  - A temporal visualizations showing how framing scores change over time, such as line charts.  
  - A visualization comparing how selected media sources score on the 5 framing dimensions, such as a clustered heatmap or grouped bar chart.  
- **Text analysis**  
  - Compare distinctive/frequent words or bigrams across article groups  
  - Topic-based comparisons showing which topics appear frequently in the full text articles and which sources emphasize them more, using topic modeling methods like BERTopic.  
- **Network analysis**  
  - *If* we embedded all articles using a sentence transformer and built a k-NN graph, what would the network tell us? (Nodes \= entities such as people, org, places; edges weighted by frequency).   
  - Frame diffusion network using a directed temporal network

Because news sites are often not clean and these highly topical words may appear in site headers, the dataset contained many irrelevant articles, and so we use a sentence similarity transformer to specifically find article titles that are similar to a set of topics that are obviously related to the Iran War, and dissimilar to a few common topics that were still appearing highly on the sentence similarity index.
